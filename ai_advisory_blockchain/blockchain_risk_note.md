# Blockchain & Crypto Risk-Analysis Appendix

## 1. Stablecoin Type & DeFi/DAO Governance Risk — A "Paytm Crypto Insights" Watchlist Assessment

A hypothetical "Paytm Crypto Insights" watchlist feature would need to clearly distinguish between **fiat-collateralized stablecoins** (e.g., USDC, USDT) and **algorithmic stablecoins** (e.g., the now-defunct UST/LUNA). Fiat-collateralized stablecoins maintain their peg through reserves of real-world assets — cash, Treasury bills, or commercial paper — held by a custodian, and their primary risk lies in reserve transparency and audit quality. Algorithmic stablecoins, by contrast, attempt to maintain their peg through programmatic supply-adjustment mechanisms and arbitrage incentives without holding equivalent fiat reserves; the Terra/LUNA collapse of May 2022 demonstrated that these mechanisms can enter a reflexive "death spiral" when confidence breaks, destroying over $40 billion in value within days.

Before Paytm could responsibly surface any crypto asset to its retail user base, the watchlist feature must implement: (a) a **stablecoin-type label** clearly marking each listed stablecoin as fiat-collateralized or algorithmic, with a risk disclaimer for the latter; (b) a **reserve audit status indicator** for fiat-collateralized coins (e.g., whether the issuer publishes monthly proof-of-reserves attestations); and (c) a **DAO governance risk score** for any DeFi token, assessing tokenomics concentration (e.g., whether fewer than 10 wallets control >50% of governance tokens, enabling unilateral protocol changes), proposal velocity, and historical governance attack incidents. The fundamental challenge with DAO governance is that token-weighted voting creates plutocratic control structures — large holders can pass proposals that extract value from smaller participants, and "flash-loan governance attacks" have exploited this in protocols like Beanstalk.

## 2. Crypto-as-an-Asset-Class Recommendation for Paytm Money

Standard CAPM-style portfolio theory does not favor including cryptocurrency in an optimal portfolio for several structural reasons:

- **No intrinsic value or cash flows:** Unlike equities (earnings/dividends) or bonds (coupons), cryptocurrencies generate no cash flows, making any fundamental valuation exercise purely speculative. CAPM's expected-return framework relies on assets with a definable relationship to market risk factors; crypto's pricing is dominated by sentiment, narrative cycles, and liquidity flows rather than systematic risk beta.
- **Heavy-tailed, positively-skewed returns:** Crypto return distributions exhibit extreme kurtosis and positive skewness — large upside moves capture attention while large drawdowns (70-90% peak-to-trough) are routine. Standard mean-variance optimization, which assumes approximately normal returns, systematically underestimates tail risk for such assets.
- **Survivorship bias:** The observed "returns" of Bitcoin and Ethereum are conditioned on the fact that these specific tokens survived; thousands of other cryptocurrencies have gone to zero, and a portfolio analysis that only considers survivors dramatically overstates the asset class's expected return.
- **Low/negative correlation with traditional assets** is often cited as a diversification benefit, but this correlation is unstable — during the 2022 market selloff, crypto correlated strongly with risk assets, precisely when diversification was most needed.
- **High transaction costs:** Spread costs, gas fees, exchange withdrawal fees, and the implicit cost of slippage in illiquid markets erode returns, particularly for retail investors.

**Recommendation:** For Paytm Money's retail advisory product, I recommend a **zero allocation** to cryptocurrency. The combination of no intrinsic value, unreliable diversification benefits, survivorship bias in historical return data, and regulatory uncertainty in India (where crypto taxation is at 30% flat with no loss offset) makes any positive allocation unjustifiable under a fiduciary advisory standard. If Paytm Money were to offer crypto exposure in the future — perhaps under a separate, clearly-labeled speculative-trading vertical — it should be capped at a maximum of 2-3% of portfolio value, ring-fenced from the advisory product, and accompanied by mandatory risk acknowledgment disclosures.

## 3. T.A.N.G. Framework Analysis — Social-Engineering Risk Vectors for a UPI/Wallet + Lending + Wealth Platform

The T.A.N.G. (Temptation / Authority / Need / Greed) framework identifies four psychological levers that social engineers exploit to manipulate victims. For a combined UPI/wallet, lending, and wealth platform like Paytm, the two most relevant vectors are:

### Vector 1: Authority

**Risk:** Fraudsters impersonate Paytm customer support agents, RBI officials, or bank representatives, contacting users via phone or SMS and claiming that the user's KYC is expiring, their account will be frozen, or a suspicious transaction requires immediate "verification." The user, recognizing the authority figure, is pressured into sharing their UPI PIN, OTP, or approving a collect request that transfers funds to the attacker. On a platform like Paytm — which spans payments, lending, and investments — the authority vector is especially dangerous because the attacker can reference any of these verticals to sound credible ("Your Paytm Postpaid EMI is overdue and your CIBIL score will be impacted" or "Your Paytm Money mutual fund redemption requires re-verification").

**Bank-side defense: Real-time behavioral biometric analysis.** The payment processor should deploy device-fingerprinting and behavioral biometrics (typing cadence, touch pressure, screen-interaction patterns) that flag when a transaction is being initiated under coaching — e.g., unusually slow PIN entry, simultaneous phone call activity, or device screen-sharing application running in the background. When detected, the system should inject a 2-minute cooling-off hold with an explicit on-screen warning ("No Paytm employee will ever ask for your PIN or OTP") before allowing the transaction to proceed.

### Vector 2: Greed

**Risk:** Users receive messages promising guaranteed high returns on crypto investments, "pre-approved" Paytm Postpaid credit limit increases requiring a small processing fee, or fake cashback offers that redirect to phishing sites. The greed vector specifically targets Paytm Money users with fabricated "insider tips" or "algorithmic trading bots" promising risk-free returns, exploiting their existing trust in the platform's investment vertical. Lending users are targeted with fake loan-approval scams demanding upfront fees.

**Bank-side defense: Transaction-purpose anomaly detection with beneficiary intelligence.** The system should maintain a real-time graph of known fraud-linked beneficiary accounts (mule accounts) and flag any first-time transfer to a beneficiary that matches known fraud-network patterns — new account, high inflow velocity, or recent fraud complaints. When a user initiates a transfer to such an account, the platform should trigger an interactive friction step requiring the user to select the purpose of the transfer from a dropdown and explicitly confirm that no one is guiding them through the transaction, reducing the completion rate of greed-motivated scam transfers by introducing deliberate cognitive friction.
